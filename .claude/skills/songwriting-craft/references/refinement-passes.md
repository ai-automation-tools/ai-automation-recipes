# Refinement Passes

Three passes over a draft, each targeting a different layer. Run them in order — tightening after
strengthening undoes the strengthening.

---

## Pass 1 — Tighten

Remove filler, compress, eliminate redundancy. Every syllable you free here buys room in the chunk budget.

| Pattern | Before | After | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Filler phrases | "He stood up and spoke the words" | "He said" | Setup phrases pad without meaning |
| Redundant modifiers | "completely destroyed" | "destroyed" | Absolute verbs need no intensifier |
| Passive voice | "The door was opened by her" | "She opened the door" | Active is more direct and more singable |
| Pronoun pile-ups | "He told him that he should go" | "Get out — the message clear" | Ambiguous pronouns weaken the image |
| Directional padding | "She looked up at the sky above" | "She searched the sky" | "Above" is doing nothing |
| Throat-clearing | "Well, I think that maybe I should" | "I should" | Commit to the statement |
| Double-saying | "alone and by myself" | "alone" | One expression per idea |

## Pass 2 — Strengthen

Upgrade imagery, sharpen sensory detail, replace generic with specific.

| Pattern | Before | After | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Generic imagery | "The city at night" | "Neon bleeding on wet asphalt" | Specific images stick; generic ones slide off |
| Abstract emotion | "I felt so lost" | "Couldn't find my keys, my name, my street" | Show the feeling through concrete detail |
| Clichés | "Cold as ice" | "Cold as a landlord's smile" | Fresh comparisons earn attention |
| Single-sense lines | "The room was dark" | "Dark — just the hum of pipes and mildew air" | Multi-sensory lines immerse |
| Weak verbs | "He went across the room" | "He cut across the room" | Strong verbs carry energy |
| Telling emotion | "She was angry" | "Her knuckles white around the glass" | Action reveals without naming |
| Vague quantity | "A lot of people came" | "Thirty faces in a room for ten" | Numbers create instant scale |

> Note the tension with Pass 1: strengthening often costs syllables. That's fine — you freed them in
> Pass 1. If Pass 2 pushes a chunk over budget, cut a *different* line rather than weakening this one.

## Pass 3 — Flow & ear

Read aloud, at tempo. This pass catches what only the mouth catches.

| Pattern | Before | After | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Consonant clusters | "Sixth street's strict structures" | "Sixth Street's sharp edges" | Tongue-trippers wreck delivery |
| Stacked diphthongs | "She oughta allow our hour" | "She gave us the hour" | Muddy at tempo |
| Transition bumps | V1 ends reflective → V2 opens aggressive | Add a pre-chorus or transitional line | Jarring shifts break the listener |
| Missing breath points | 12 syllables, no natural pause | Split at the caesura, or add a comma | Singers need air; dense lines get rushed |
| Stress misalignment | "into the DARK-ness" on a weak beat | "the DARKness CALLS" on strong beats | Stressed syllables must land on downbeats |
| Choppy rhythm | "Stop. Go. Wait. No. Run." | "Stop and go — wait, no — run" | Staccato lines need connective tissue |
| Syllable mismatch | V1 line 8 syllables, V2 line 13 | Match within ±2 | Parallel lines share a melodic phrase |
