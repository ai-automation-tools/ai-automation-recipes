# Rhyme & Structure

Rhyme technique, per-genre schemes, line length, and section length caps.

---

## Rhyme types — use variety

| Type | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Perfect | Exact match | love / dove |
| Slant / Near | Similar, not exact | love / move |
| Consonance | Same ending consonants | blank / think |
| Assonance | Same vowel sounds | lake / fate |
| Internal | Rhymes inside a line | "fire and desire higher" |

## Scheme patterns

| Pattern | Effect |
|---|---|
| AABB | Stable, immediate resolution |
| ABAB | Classic, delayed resolution |
| ABCB | Lighter, less pressure |
| AAAX | Strong setup, surprise ending |

---

## Schemes by genre

**There is no universal default.** Pick from the genre in the job's brief.

| Genre family | Default scheme | Strictness | Key difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hip-Hop / Rap** | AABB (couplet) | High — multisyllabic + internal rhyme expected | Rhyme density throughout the bar, not just end rhymes |
| **Pop** | XAXA (conversational) | Low — near rhymes preferred | If it sounds "crafted," it fails |
| **Rock / Alt-Rock** | XAXA or ABAB | Low — meaning > rhyme | Imagery and emotional energy over technical rhyming |
| **Punk** | AABB (loose) | Low — half-rhymes authentic | Direct, shoutable, works at 150+ BPM |
| **Metal** | Optional | Very low — can skip entirely | Concrete imagery and riff alignment over rhyme |
| **Country / Folk** | ABCB (ballad stanza) | Moderate — near rhymes fine | Storytelling; lines 2 & 4 rhyme, 1 & 3 free |
| **Comedy Country** | AABB or ABCB | Moderate — the rhyme *is* the joke | Setup/payoff couplets; a groan-worthy rhyme is a feature |
| **Blues** | AAB (3-line) | Moderate | Line 1 states, line 2 repeats, line 3 resolves |
| **Electronic / EDM** | Repetition > rhyme | Minimal | Single phrases looped, not verses |
| **Psychedelic / Ambient / Lo-Fi** | Loose or none | Very low | Vocals are texture; repetition carries meaning |
| **Trip-Hop** | XAXA (loose) | Low | Abstract, moody |
| **R&B / Soul** | Flexible | Low — emotion first | Leave space for melisma and vocal runs |
| **Funk** | Minimal | Very low | Groove lock; lyrics accent the downbeat |
| **Gospel** | Repetitive build | Low | Call-and-response; repetition builds intensity |
| **Jazz** | AABA (32-bar) | Sophisticated | Internal rhyme, wordplay, phrasing behind/ahead of the beat |
| **Reggae / Dancehall** | Riddim-driven | Moderate | Groove lock; audience participation by design |
| **Afrobeats** | Call-and-response | Low | Code-switching is idiomatic |
| **Ballad (any genre)** | ABCB or ABAB | Moderate | Emotion and narrative serve the story |

## Quality standards — all genres

- **Never force a rhyme.** Don't bend grammar, invent words, or add filler to land one.
- **No self-rhymes.** Never rhyme a word with itself.
- **No lazy repeats.** Avoid near-identical pairs (mind/mind, time/time).
- **Meaning outranks rhyme.** If the perfect rhyme sounds unnatural, use a near rhyme or restructure.
- **Consistency within a section.** No switching scheme mid-verse.

## Anti-patterns

- ❌ Wrong scheme for the genre (hip-hop couplets in a folk ballad)
- ❌ Forcing perfect rhymes where near rhymes sound more natural
- ❌ Filler lines that exist only to set up a quote ("he stood up and spoke the words")
- ❌ Ending a verse on a line whose rhyme partner never arrives
- ❌ Inconsistent line lengths that break the vocal pocket
- ❌ Clichés: *cold as ice, broke my heart, by my side, set me free, learning to fly*, *tonight* as an end-rhyme
- ❌ Telling instead of showing ("I was angry")
- ❌ Generic abstraction where a specific image would land

## Line length

| Genre | Syllables / line | Tolerance |
|---|---|---|
| Pop / Folk / Punk | 6–8 | ±2 |
| Rock / Indie | 8–10 | ±2 |
| Hip-Hop / Rap | 10–13+ | ±3 |
| Psychedelic / Ambient | 4–8 (short, airy) | ±2 |
| Metal / Electronic | Varies | Flexible |

**Critical:** Verse 1's line lengths must match Verse 2's. They share a melody.

---

## Structure defaults

- **Default shape:** 2–3 verses + chorus + bridge.
- **Chorus:** 4–6 lines, repeated **verbatim**.
- **Pre-chorus:** 2–4 lines. The most efficient way to add duration without bloating a section.
- **Bridge:** 2–4 lines. A fresh perspective or emotional turn before the final chorus.
- **Outro:** optional, 2–4 lines. Not a new verse.
- **Instrumental breaks:** a chunk with no sung text — adds runtime at zero syllable cost.

**To hit a longer duration, add sections — never lengthen them.** In priority order: add a third
verse, add a pre-chorus before each chorus, add an instrumental break, add a bridge if missing.

## Enforcement

1. Count lines per section after drafting; compare to the table.
2. Over the cap → trim it. Don't ask, cut.
3. Rap verse over 8 lines → split into two verses or cut. No exceptions.
4. Any chorus over 6 lines → trim. A long chorus loses its punch.
5. Electronic or psychedelic verse over its cap → cut. These genres need space.
6. Punk over its caps → it isn't punk any more.
7. Genre unclear → use the Pop/Rock defaults (6–8 verse, 4–6 chorus, 4 bridge).
8. **Also check the BPM-aware limits** in `density-and-duration.md` — a genre may allow an 8-line
   verse at fast tempo but only 4 at slow tempo. The stricter of the two wins.
